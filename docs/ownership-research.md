# Ownership and contract research — current model 1.4.0

Research conducted October 5, 2026 (retrieval records use UTC). All new source records S28–S33 and claims C35–C40 are agent-reviewed, not human-verified. Public prices and reports are benchmarks, not delivered offers. The financial ranking is still conditional.

## Construction
JLL's 2026 Global Data Center Outlook, page 25, forecasts global shell/core construction at USD11.3m/MW, with a 10% liquid-cooling premium. Applied calculation: 11.3 × 1.10 = USD12.43m/MW IT. JLL's benchmark is a single-tenant 50MW air-cooled facility; land and active IT are excluded. Using it for the 20MW concept and all three regions is an explicitly shared global proxy. The independent grid-upgrade allowance is retained as external utility work, not internal distribution. Local conditions, land, fees and actual scope remain unpriced. ±20% construction stresses are analyst choices, not JLL confidence intervals. This replaces the unsupported USD8m/MW default.

## Power and performance
NVIDIA's DGX H100 datasheet rates an eight-GPU system at about 10.2kW maximum; 10.2/8 = 1.275kW/GPU including node overhead. Retain conservative 1.4kW allocation; test 1.275 with the same annual demand. An empirical H100 node study reports idle 1.86kW and maximum measured 8.48kW; compare idle/rated 18.2% with the 35% planning profile. These are different configurations, not measured performance of the proposed facility. Rated power, active power, GPU utilization and delivered application throughput are distinct. No universal conversion factor is supported. The original 95% productive rental conversion remains an assumption, with fixed tests at 70% and 100%; these are analyst bounds, not measurements.

## Contracts and additional costs
Nebius charges running compute and stops compute charges on stopped VMs, while volumes remain billed. AWS running instances are billed even when idle; stopped compute is not billed, but resources such as EBS and reserved obligations remain. Azure stopped allocated VMs remain billed until deallocated; disks/networking can continue charges. Baseline assumes actual compute release between allocations, not merely an idle GPU. Reallocation capacity is not guaranteed. The retained-allocation diagnostic bills at least the annual forecast leased footprint, even when demand falls, at the same public price: no invented discount or actual supplier commitment. It represents hypothetical rolling one-year reservations. One-year forecast lease obligation at the decision date is included in its capital exposure proxy without adding it again to economic cash cost. Actual upfront payments and longer obligations are not modeled. A 30% service allowance tests storage/support/egress against the 10% baseline; neither is a supplier breakdown.

## Fixed robustness diagnostics
Fifteen presets, each recalculating nine country/strategy combinations and all three required cases. No additional user controls, optimizer, probabilities or opaque weighted score. Tests include construction ±20%, published server configurations, productive conversion 70/100%, retained rental allocation, rated-node power, measured-node idle reference, five-year refresh, 30% service allowance, Québec LG reference and two joint stresses. Global annual demand remains fixed (half in the required half-demand case). Existing LG eligibility remains unconfirmed; demand-charge calculation assumes 74% load factor.

Benchmark preference remains Finland–HYBRID, but tested base preferences include Finland–BUILD and Finland–LEASE. Status: ASSUMPTION-SENSITIVE. No robust strategy winner is claimed. The tests do not prove Finland dominates untested supplier, regional-cost, performance or feasibility variations. Signed member demand, comparable workload benchmarks, site-specific utility offers, delivered quotes and lender terms remain approval gates.
